If your 14-year-old asks to open a social media account, the EU KIDS Act would give some platforms the option to offer a limited account that you set up and manage. Independent accounts on covered social networks and video-sharing platforms would start at 15. The European Commission presented the proposal on 17 September 2026. As of 17 November 2026, it was still under review and creates no new obligations for families today. [Commission proposal][proposal] · [Legislative tracker][tracker]

## What would change at 13 and 15?

The proposed age rules apply to accounts on social networks and video-sharing platforms with features the draft identifies as risky, such as public livestreaming, recommendations based on profiling, or contact with people outside your existing connections. [Draft, Article 6][draft]

- **Under 13.** Children would be ineligible for their own accounts. A narrow exception would let children aged 3–12 use a video service designed for that age group through a guardian’s account, subject to safeguards.
- **Ages 13–14.** A platform could offer a limited account set up by a verified parent or legal guardian. Parental tools would stay on. The adult could approve contacts and set a daily limit of at most one hour on that service.
- **Ages 15–17.** Teenagers could open their own accounts under the proposed EU threshold. Platforms would still have to apply protections for minors, and individual services could have a higher minimum age.

For a teen who already has an account, the Commission’s draft gives covered providers six months from the date the rules start applying to check existing accounts. It requires them to disable accounts found to be under the threshold or whose age they cannot establish. [Draft, Articles 6–7 and 32][draft]

## What would change inside the apps?

**Social feeds and messages.** Covered platforms would have to start minors off with more private settings, including location sharing off by default. They would have to limit unsolicited contact, give teens more control over recommendations, and curb features that encourage compulsive use, such as autoplay without meaningful breaks or streaks that penalize a missed day. [Draft, Articles 9–12][draft]

**Games, chatbots and app stores.** Children under 13 would need parental controls to access covered online games, general-purpose chatbots, AI companions and app stores. Games would face safeguards for privacy and contact, and chatbots would face restrictions on features likely to foster emotional dependence. App stores would have to age-rate the apps they carry and restrict access to apps unsuitable for a minor’s age. [Draft, Articles 14–16][draft]

## How would platforms check age?

For new accounts subject to the social media threshold, the draft calls for certified age verification. The Commission describes a system where a platform receives proof that you meet an age threshold without receiving your ID document or exact birth date. Each EU country would have to make at least one certified option available free of charge. The draft also provides a way to challenge an incorrect result. [Commission FAQ][faq] · [Draft, Articles 28–32][draft]

One question for families is how they would keep access if a guardian is unavailable or a check goes wrong. Eurochild has proposed letting 13- and 14-year-olds hold limited accounts independently, with the safety restrictions enforced by the platform. It points to the needs of children in alternative care and those without an available guardian. [Eurochild’s response][eurochild]

## What can parents do now?

Open one app together. Check who can contact your child, what the app shares about their location, and where they can block or report someone. Our [TikTok](/en/apps/tiktok), [YouTube](/en/apps/youtube) and [Roblox](/en/apps/roblox) guides walk through the settings families can use today. Ask your child which friends or communities they would want to stay in touch with if their account options changed.

The European Parliament and Council still have to agree on a final text. EU ministers are scheduled to discuss the proposal on 20 November 2026. The Commission lists 3 December 2026 as the deadline for public feedback. [Council agenda][council] · [Commission consultation][feedback]
